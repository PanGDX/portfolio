### TLDR;

- In a team of three, built a robot for remote navigation in unknown, obstacle-filled rooms.
- Implemented a simple Real-Time Operating System (RTOS) and integrated Lidar for environment mapping.
- Developed a CLI to send low-latency commands for remote control.
- The robot successfully generated a 2D map of its surroundings for the remote operator.


### Problem
This project attempted to simulate the circumstance of a rescue robot. A rescue robot would be operating in difficult and unknown terrain. The operator will not be able to know the layout of the terrain beforehand and have to use sensors attached to the robot to navigate smoothly.
The mission is to map out the terrain and 'rescue' key objectives - a red and a green bottle - using the claws equipped. We were to control from another room without any visibility. After the fact, we had to draw out the map of the terrain by hand based on the SLAM.

### Solution

To simulate a real-world rescue mission in a high-stakes, zero-visibility environment, our team developed a robust system architecture that unified real-time processing, low-latency communication, and precise mechanical engineering. The solution focused on turning a mobile chassis into a sophisticated mapping and retrieval unit.

#### 1. Real-Time Operating System (RTOS) and Task Management
To ensure the robot remained responsive while handling multiple high-frequency data streams, we implemented a **simple Real-Time Operating System (RTOS)**. This allowed us to manage concurrency through task prioritization:
*   **Time-Critical Tasks:** Motor control and command processing were assigned high priority to ensure the robot could stop or turn instantly, preventing collisions.
*   **Data-Heavy Tasks:** Lidar scanning and telemetry transmission were handled as background tasks, preventing the "bottlenecking" of the CPU while processing thousands of laser data points per second.

#### 2. Lidar Integration and Environmental Mapping
The core of our "blind" navigation capability was the **RPLidar** integration. 
*   **Data Processing:** We programmed the system to ingest raw polar coordinate data (distance and angle) and convert it into a Cartesian $(x, y)$ map in real-time.
*   **SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping):** By streaming this data to the remote operator’s terminal, we were able to perform a simplified version of SLAM. This enabled the operator to "see" walls, obstacles, and the exact position of the "rescue" objectives (red and green bottles) without a direct line of sight.

#### 3. Low-Latency Remote Control System (Python/C++ Hybrid)
Operating from a separate room required a communication stack with minimal lag. We developed a hybrid system to achieve this:
*   **Python Command Interface:** On the operator’s end, we built a Command Line Interface (CLI) in Python for its flexibility and ease of data visualization.
*   **C++ Hardware Execution:** On the robot’s firmware, we used C++ to interact with the motors and sensors at a low level, ensuring maximum execution speed and minimal overhead.
*   **Communication Protocol:** We optimized the packet structure sent between the laptop and the robot to ensure that commands (forward, reverse, turn, claw actuate) were received and executed in near-real-time, which was critical for navigating tight corners in unknown terrain.

#### 4. Mechanical Design of the Rescue Claw
The "rescue" aspect of the mission required a physical solution for retrieving objects. We iterated on the **claw design** to find the perfect balance between reliability and weight.
*   **Precision and Grip:** The claw was engineered to be precise enough to grasp plastic bottles without tipping them over and strong enough to maintain a hold during high-torque turns.
*   **Compact Integration:** We optimized the design to ensure it didn't interfere with the Lidar’s 360-degree field of vision, avoiding "blind spots" in our generated map.

#### 5. Blind Navigation and Post-Mission Synthesis
The ultimate test was the "black-box" operation. By relying solely on the CLI feedback and the Lidar-generated telemetry, we navigated the obstacle course to identify and retrieve the targets. After the mission, we used the digital data collected by the robot to reconstruct a precise, hand-drawn map of the terrain, demonstrating the accuracy of our SLAM implementation and the reliability of our remote-control stack.
